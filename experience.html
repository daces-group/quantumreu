10weeks of discovery

A residential research experience

The program is based in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, with connections to IQUIST, the Coordinated Science Laboratory, and campus computing resources.

The work emphasizes theory, algorithms, and reproducible computation. You can contribute through simulations, mathematical reasoning, visualization, or protocol analysis; specialized quantum hardware is not required for every project.

Your research pathway

From a first question
to a defensible result.

The schedule below describes the planned research progression. Detailed daily schedules and partner activities will be shared with participants.

  1. Before arrival

    Build the foundations

    Short bridge modules in Python/NumPy, complex vectors, probability, and scientific notebooks; a mentor introduction and software setup.

  2. Week 1

    Meet your research community

    Orientation, responsible research, quantum-network bootcamp, computing safety, and exploration of project directions.

  3. Week 2

    Make the question your own

    Map the literature, create a project charter and reproducibility plan, and develop a mentoring compact and Individual Development Plan.

  4. Weeks 3–4

    Reproduce, explain, then vary

    Replicate a known result, begin guided implementation or derivation, and test the assumptions behind your first research direction.

  5. Week 5

    Pause, present, and refine

    Give a midpoint talk and write a short memo. Use mentor and peer feedback to calibrate the scope and the next experiments.

  6. Weeks 6–7

    Work with greater independence

    Test hypotheses, compare protocols, debug models, or develop proofs, alongside career and graduate-school programming.

  7. Weeks 8–9

    Check the evidence. Tell the story.

    Complete robustness checks and peer reproduction, then develop your paper, poster, and accessible research explanation.

  8. Week 10

    Share what you discovered

    Present at the final symposium, submit your research package, and create a continuation plan with your mentor.

  9. After the summer

    Keep the connection

    The mentoring plan includes follow-ups approximately six weeks and four months after the program, with next steps tailored to your project and goals.

Your research portfolio

Leave with a result
you can stand behind.

  • A research poster and an oral presentation
  • A short technical paper or extended abstract
  • Reproducible code, notebooks, or a derivation package
  • A continuation plan for research, coursework, or your next career step

Research does not promise a predetermined positive outcome. Clear evidence, sound methods, and explicit limitations are central to a successful project.
